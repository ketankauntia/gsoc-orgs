# Product Blueprint

## Positioning

**GSoC Atlas helps contributors turn a large archive of organizations and
projects into a focused, evidence-backed shortlist.**

It is an independent research tool, not an official Google or GSoC property.

## Primary audiences

1. A first-time contributor who does not know where to begin.
2. A returning contributor validating a small set of organizations.
3. A mentor or community member studying program history.
4. A researcher or publisher using the public data and API.

## Core journeys

### Explore

Search by organization, technology, or topic; apply understandable filters; scan
compact evidence; open a promising organization.

Success: a user reaches a relevant organization detail page without horizontal
scrolling or losing filter state.

### Evaluate

Understand current participation, history, projects, technologies, contribution
links, ideas links, and data freshness.

Success: a user can explain why an organization is or is not a fit using sourced
facts—not a synthetic “score.”

### Understand

Move between yearly, technology, topic, project, and organization evidence.

Success: charts state their takeaway and link to the underlying entities.

### Prepare

Use verified guides and, later, a local shortlist and proposal workspace.

Success: every guide offers a relevant next product action rather than becoming a
content dead end.

## Public information architecture

- **Explore**
  - Organizations
  - Projects
  - Technologies
  - Topics
- **Insights**
  - Yearly
  - Data methodology
  - API
- **Prepare**
  - Guides
  - Blog
  - Future proposal workspace
- **Trust**
  - About
  - Changelog
  - Contact
  - Data freshness and limitations

Existing URLs remain stable. Navigation labels and cross-links provide the new
hierarchy without an immediate route migration.

## Landing-page argument

1. Promise: Google Summer of Code, mapped.
2. Action: search the dataset or explore organizations.
3. Proof: real organization/project/active-organization counts and snapshot date.
4. Paths: start from a stack, domain, first recorded participation, or year.
5. Product demo: compact organization evidence inside an explorer frame.
6. Method: explain where the data comes from and what it cannot prove.
7. Journey: discover → validate → shortlist → prepare.
8. Learn: show real published guides.
9. Roadmap: label AI and proposal upload as future.
10. Final action: open the organization explorer.

## Feature order

### Now

- Design system and unified public shell
- Landing page
- Organization discovery and detail clarity
- Analytics framing and chart accessibility
- Unified blog identity and cross-links
- Truthful freshness/method labels

### Next

- Local-first shortlist
- Side-by-side organization compare with explainable fields
- Taxonomy aliases and curated topic families
- CSV export of filtered results
- Shared insights hub

### Later, behind gates

- Citation-backed AI briefs
- Proposal upload, moderation, consent, and review workspace
- Accounts and synced shortlists
